|---|---|---|
| nhm-brachiosaurus | Museum page | 22 m, 46,900 kg (method not stated); Late Jurassic, 152–145 Ma; no complete skeleton, most reconstructions based on *Giraffatitan*; first remains found in Colorado in 1900 by Elmer S. Riggs, who first thought it a *Brontosaurus*; named 1903; "arm lizard": front legs longer than back legs, upward-tilted body; probably fed on tall trees (conifers, tree ferns), swallowed food whole, hindgut fermentation (level 3); holotype possibly not fully grown; lived with *Brontosaurus*, much taller; taxonomy |
| taylor-2009 | Abstract + author's open copy | Popular image based on *Giraffatitan*; at least 26 distinguishing characters; *Giraffatitan* must be a separate genus; longer and deeper trunk, probably longer and taller tail, more mass on the forelimbs; holotype probably immature (coracoids unfused); holotype FMNH P 25107 from the Grand River valley of western Colorado, 1900 Field Columbian Museum expedition under Riggs; holotype contents (last seven dorsals, sacrum, two caudals, coracoid, humerus, ilium, femur, ribs; no skull or neck); Dry Mesa ("*Ultrasauros*") material not referable, probably smaller than the holotype; GDI mass 28,688 kg at 0.8 kg/l |

## Measurements

Mass shows all three estimates, 28.7–56.3 t, and "What remains uncertain" explains the factor-of-two spread (body model vs. limb-bone scaling). Taylor also quotes Paul (1988) at 35 t; not cited second-hand. Length is the museum figure; the 3D model is scaled so its backbone, traced from snout to tail tip on the silhouette, measures 22 m.

## Not used

- Paul 1988 (*Hunteria*): not read directly.
- Skull shape of the model: the model follows the usual *Giraffatitan*-based image; the referred skull's shape was not compared, so no claim is made about the model's snout.
